## What gyms offer

- Predictable weather — train any season.
- User-friendly holds; routes change weekly.
- Padded floors and crash pads for [[bouldering]].
- Hangboards, campus boards, system walls for [[training]].
- Lessons, beginner classes, and a social hub.

Modern gyms have giant overhanging walls, slabs, arêtes, crack-system simulators, top-rope and lead lines, and specialized training rooms.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 290)

## What gyms train well

- **Movement, technique, footwork** — large volume in low-risk environment.
- **Power, endurance** — bouldering walls and routes.
- **Lead-clipping confidence** — every gym has clip-and-go lead lines.
- **Belay skill** — gym instructors watch every belay; hundreds of catches in a few weeks builds the reflex.

## The gym-to-outdoor trap

The book's warning: a gym-trained climber faces a *rude awakening* when she ventures outside (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 292):

- **Weather** — rain, cold, heat.
- **Loose rock** — a fact of nature; gyms have none.
- **Holds breaking** — outdoor crystals can pop.
- **Route finding** — gym holds are color-coded; outdoor routes are not.
- **Protection** — gyms have bolts every 6 ft; outdoor climbs may have nothing.
- **Self-rescue** — gyms have staff; outdoor climbers have themselves.

The recommendation: go outside first with an experienced friend or a certified guide.

## Holds and route setting

Climbing holds are cast of epoxy resin with grit added. Modular — bolt to a wall in any position. Route setting is an art form; the best gyms employ dedicated setters who design master-class routes weekly (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 290).

## Common gym injuries

- **Crimp injuries** — sequences of crimps on vertical or sub-vertical walls strain finger pulleys.
- **Tweaks** — small tendon strains from cold pulling.
- **Shoulder rotator-cuff** — from steep climbing without antagonist training.

Warm up properly. Skip the "crimp fest" warm-up.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 290)

## Etiquette

- Yield to climbers on lead.
- Don't loiter at the base of a popular route.
- Brush chalk off shared holds.
- Don't walk through someone's fall zone.

## Choosing a gym

The book recommends taking a few hours to scout: route variety, route freshness, bouldering quality, lead-line length, training-room quality, classes, community vibe. Any moderately serious climber should join one (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 292).
