## Construction

Standard: **18 feet of 7 mm nylon cord** (joined with a double fisherman's knot) or **5.5 mm high-tensile cord** (joined with a triple fisherman's knot). Forms a closed loop ~9 feet long when doubled (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 195).

7 mm nylon is more forgiving — it stretches under load and helps equalize unequal arms. 5.5 mm high-tensile is more compact but stiffer and less forgiving.

## Rigging

1. Clip the cordelette through each of 2–4 primary placements.
2. Pull the loops *down* between each pair of pieces.
3. Pull all loops tight in the anticipated [[direction-of-pull]].
4. Tie an overhand or figure-eight on a bight to form the **power point** (about 4 inches in diameter, similar to the harness belay loop).
5. Position the fisherman's join *below the highest piece* so it doesn't interfere with the power point knot.
6. Clip two locking biners (or two opposed regular biners) into the power point.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 196)

## Why a cordelette

- **One piece of gear** equalizes 2–4 placements.
- **Familiar everywhere**: the standard trad rigging tool for two decades.
- **Compact** — racks on the harness.
- **Single power point** for tying in the second and the leader cleanly.
- Eliminates the snake-pit of separate slings between every pair of placements.

## Limitations

- **Static equalization** — distributes load only in the anticipated direction. Off-axis loads (5–10° away) place most of the force on a single arm (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 199).
- **Unequal arms** = poor equalization. Lab tests show the shortest arm takes most of the load when arms are unequal (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 195).
- Best with **2 placements** when an [[rigging-systems|equalette]] becomes more appropriate for 3–4.

## When to choose a cordelette

- Two side-by-side bolts or pieces at roughly equal distance.
- Direction of pull is predictable (a straight-up-and-down trad route).
- Time pressure.
- Want a single power point quickly.

## When to choose something else

- **3–4 placements with unequal arms** → [[rigging-systems|equalette]]
- **Two side-by-side bolts on a sport top-anchor** → [[rigging-systems|quad]]
- **Direction of pull uncertain** → equalette or sliding X with limiter knots
- **Two pieces only, on the leader's lead** → sliding X

## Cordelette + Sliding X composite

A common combination: equalize two pieces with a sliding X, then add a third piece via the cordelette to the power point. Combines the dynamic equalization of the X with the cordelette's pre-tensioned third arm (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 207).
