## What it is

By definition, you are free soloing **any time you climb without a rope, regardless of the difficulty** (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 245). Common usage restricts the term to climbing fifth-class terrain ropeless.

The free soloist carries:
- Pair of shoes.
- Chalk bag.
- Skill, focus, and judgment.

Nothing else.

## Why people do it

The book's framing: not because they are reckless, but because the *intensity* and *freedom* of climbing without the rope is incomparable. The mental focus the practice demands is described as a kind of mastery.

The reasons must come from the heart, not from external validation. If a soloist is motivated by vanity, "he might pay for his vanity" (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 245).

## The math

A 5.14 climber falling off 5.10 terrain is rare. Most soloing is done on routes the soloist has previously climbed with a rope — rehearsed, wired, with familiarity removing the doubt that drives a fall.

Pure on-sight free soloing beyond mid-5.11 is now exceptional. Most "free solos" are well-rehearsed ascents (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 245).

## Statistics

Free soloing accidents are *rare*, because:
- The practitioners are an elite minority.
- The practice is undertaken in a "measured, sober way" by those still alive to discuss it.

When accidents happen, "an obituary soon follows" (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 245).

## The book's stance

> Ultimately, free soloing is a distinctively personal affair, and even daily practitioners discourage the practice, as they should. There is certainly no reason for the recreational climber to ever even consider it.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 245)

The author's position: present the practice honestly, but unambiguously discourage it for anyone reading a beginner-to-intermediate climbing manual.

## Peter Croft, Alex Honnold, Dean Potter

The book's photo on p. 244 features **Peter Croft** on *Loose Lips* (5.11a), Joshua Tree, 1987 — one of the era's most respected free soloists. Croft, Dean Potter, and Alex Honnold (whose era largely postdates this 2010 edition) became the public faces of high-end free soloing.
