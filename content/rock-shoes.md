## Anatomy

- **Upper** — leather (stretches), synthetic (doesn't stretch), or hybrid.
- **Last** — the foot-shaped form the shoe is built around. Board-lasted = stiff; slip-lasted = sensitive.
- **Rand** — rubber wrapping the toe and forefoot for friction on edges and smears.
- **Sole** — the sticky rubber on the bottom; 4–5 mm thick.
- **Heel** — molded rubber cup for [[footwork|heel hooks]].
- **Closure** — laces (precise fit), velcro (fast), slipper (slip-on, very sensitive).

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 10–12)

## Categories

| Type | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Comfort / trad** | Flat last, leather, laces. All-day comfort. For trad multi-pitch. |
| **All-around** | Slight downturn, moderate stiffness. The default first shoe. |
| **Aggressive / sport** | Downturned toe, asymmetric, slip-on. For steep sport and bouldering. |
| **Slipper** | No closure, very thin sole, board-lasted. For [[crack-climbing|cracks]] and slabs. |
| **Approach shoe** | Hybrid shoe with climbing rubber on the toe. For getting *to* the climb. |

## Fitting

The book's classical advice:

- **Snug, not painful.** Toes flat or barely curled. Tight enough that the foot doesn't move inside the shoe.
- **Leather stretches**; size more aggressively if leather.
- **Synthetic doesn't stretch**; size with current comfort.
- Most beginners size too small. A shoe that pinches you stupid on a 30-ft climb won't last 200 ft of crack.

## Shoes for cracks

Stiffer, thicker-soled shoes — sometimes a board-lasted slip-lasted slipper — protect the foot in thin cracks. Soft slippers crumple under torque and hurt (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 83).

## Resoling

Rubber wears out at the toe. Resoling is cheaper than replacement and extends the shoe's life 2–4×. Most major cities have resolers.

## Brands

The book is brand-agnostic. Major brands as of 2010: La Sportiva, Five Ten, Scarpa, Mad Rock, Boreal, Evolv, Red Chili, Tenaya.

## Walking around in climbing shoes

**Don't.** Climbing rubber wears down fast on dirt, sand, and concrete. Experienced climbers wear approach shoes between the cliff and the parking lot, and only put on climbing shoes at the base of the route (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 221).
