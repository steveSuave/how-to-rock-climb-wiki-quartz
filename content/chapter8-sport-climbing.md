## What sport climbing is

[[sport-climbing|Sport climbing]] is climbing on routes protected exclusively by bolts placed roughly a body length apart. The leader carries shoes, harness, rope, and a handful of [[carabiners|quickdraws]] — no rack of nuts or cams. Most sport routes are ≤100 ft (one rope length), and the leader lowers off the top anchor (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 273).

By engineering the gear hazards away, sport climbing lets the climber focus on the moves. The trade-off: sport routes are typically steep to overhanging, and falls are quick and hard.

## Life at the crag

The standard ritual: scope the route, rack the draws, climb on, calm under pressure, clip every bolt with the gate facing away from travel, reach the chain anchors, clip in with sling + locker, lean back, get lowered (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 274).

## Communication

A leader on a sport climb should *never* call "off belay" at the chains if she intends to lower — the belayer needs to keep her on the rope (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 274). Good comm prevents the most common sport-climbing fatality: belayer takes climber off, climber leans back, falls to the deck.

## Lowering

The first rule: **never lower with the rope running through a nylon sling, cord, or aluminum rap ring** — friction will saw through (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 275). Lower only through steel chain links, welded cold shuts, or carabiners.

Belayer's job: two hands on the brake side, rope stacked in a bag, lower steady, not too slow (slow lowering builds extra friction on the anchor).

The standard cleaning sequence at the chains (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 276):
1. Clip in with sling + locker to the anchors.
2. Pull a bight, tie a figure-eight, clip into your belay loop.
3. Untie original tie-in, thread through chains, retie.
4. Backup, dismantle slings, lower.

## Cleaning the route

The lower-off leader "cleans" — removes the quickdraws — as she descends. On overhanging routes, "tramming" down the loaded rope (clipping into the lowering strand on each draw) prevents her from swinging out of reach (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 278).

## Honing up

Sport ascent goals (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 279):
- **Hangdog** — rehearse hanging on the rope.
- **Redpoint** — clean lead from the ground, no rests.
- **Pinkpoint** — clean lead with draws pre-placed.

For improvement: rotate doable, near-limit, at-limit, and beyond-limit climbs; consistency twice or thrice a week (one in the gym); cross-train; recovery > training every other day.

The real 5.12 threshold is 5.11c — break through that and 5.12 starts to come (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 281).

## Style

Maxed-out leaders rehearse moves while hangdogging. As soon as the rope is weighted, the on-sight is dead and a redpoint becomes the goal. Honest reporting is the climbing world's only ethic enforcement mechanism (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 279).
