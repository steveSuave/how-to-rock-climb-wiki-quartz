## The two fundamental placements

### Smearing
The whole rubber sole pressed flat against the rock. Used on slabs, friction, and rounded features. Drop the heel to maximize rubber-to-rock contact. Weight stacks directly through the smear (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 19–21).

### Edging
The shoe's edge on a small ledge or crystal. Two variants:
- **Inside edge** — big-toe side. The standard. Strong, precise.
- **Outside edge** — pinky side. Used with backstep / drop knee. Lets the hip turn into the wall.

The "micro-edge" is precise placement on a crystal smaller than the shoe edge (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 38).

## Body position techniques

### Backstep
Hip turned to the wall; outside edge of the foot pressing on a hold *behind* (relative to the direction of pull). The free side of the body extends out — flagging — for balance. The closer the hips to the wall, the lighter the load on the arms (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 23–24).

### Drop knee
A more aggressive backstep: the knee drops *down and inward*. Used on steep, overhanging climbing to bring the hip in tight to the wall. Frees a hand for a long reach.

### Heel hooking
Foot lifted high, heel grabbed over an edge, pulled toward the body like a third hand. Critical on roof problems and any move that requires pulling instead of pushing (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 26–28).

### Toeing-in
Pointing the toe straight onto a hold; especially useful in small pockets (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 23).

## The steps

### Rest step
Move one foot up to a high hold, weight onto it, free the other foot, set it; weight balanced over both before the next hand move. Slow, balanced, low-energy (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 25).

### Static step
Weight on one leg while the other rises to the next hold. The "rock-over" — straight-leg push up.

### Spring step
Both feet "bounce" off the lower hold to propel weight upward.

### Frog step (bullfrog)
Bring one foot up, then the other, while keeping the torso at the same level. Forms a crouched position before standing into the next move (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 308 glossary).

## Universal rules

1. *Place* the foot — don't drop it on. The eye watches the hold to the moment of contact.
2. *Hold* the foot — once placed, don't shift; rolling rubber slips.
3. *Trust* the foot — push down through the heel.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 17–18)
