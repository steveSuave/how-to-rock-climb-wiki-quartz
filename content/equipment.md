## Entry kit (gym / first outdoor trip)

The bare minimum to start (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 10–15):

- **Rock shoes** — see [[rock-shoes]].
- **Chalk bag** with magnesium carbonate chalk.
- **Sit [[harness-and-tie-in|harness]]** with belay loop.
- **Helmet** — outdoors, always.
- **Locking biner** — for the [[belay-device]].
- **[[belay-device]]** — ATC or Reverso.

Total cost ~$200–300 in 2010 dollars.

## Sport rack (add to entry kit)

For sport leading on bolts (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 273):

- **10–15 [[carabiners|quickdraws]]**.
- **60 m or 70 m [[rope]]** (9.2–9.9 mm).
- **60 cm sling + 2 locking biners** for the top anchor.

## Trad rack (add to sport rack)

For traditional climbing with placed gear (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 218):

- **Full set of wired [[passive-protection|nuts]]** — micro to 1".
- **Progressively sized [[camming-devices|cams]]** — finger-size to 2–3".
- **5–6 shoulder-length runners**.
- **Locking biners** for anchors.
- **Nut tool** — flat metal hook for prying stuck nuts.
- Optional: [[passive-protection|hexes]], [[passive-protection|Tricams]], [[passive-protection|Big Bros]] for wide cracks.

## Multipitch / alpine additions

- **Second rope** (for [[rappel]] descent).
- **[[knots|Prusik]] cord** — 6–7 mm, 4 ft length.
- **Cordelette** — 18 ft of 7 mm cord.
- **Headlamp**.
- **Water and food**.
- **Lightweight rain jacket**.

## Helmets

Modern climbing helmets weigh 8–12 oz. Two types:
- **Hardshell** — durable, heavier, cheaper.
- **Foam-core / hybrid** — lighter, more comfortable, less durable.

Always wear one outdoors. The book is unambiguous: a helmet protects against rockfall, gear droppage, flipping in a fall, hitting a ledge or wall (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 14–15).

## Chalk

Magnesium carbonate (gymnast chalk) absorbs sweat from the fingers. Sold loose, in balls, or in liquid form (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 12–14).

- Use sparingly — chalk graffiti detracts from the rock and is increasingly banned at sensitive areas (Hueco Tanks, Joshua Tree).
- Carry a brush to clean holds.
- "Liquid chalk" (alcohol-based) leaves less residue.

## What not to skimp on

- **[[harness-and-tie-in|Harness]]** — your only connection to the rope. Skip the cheap ones.
- **[[rope]]** — buy UIAA-tested only. Replace after a major fall.
- **[[belay-device]]** — get a major brand (BD, Petzl, Trango).
- **Helmet** — don't go without.

## What you can skimp on initially

- **[[camming-devices|Cams]]** — most expensive piece. Borrow at first; supplement with [[passive-protection|hexes]] until you can afford the cam rack.
