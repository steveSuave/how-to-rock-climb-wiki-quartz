## What bouldering is

Climbing a sequence of moves — a **problem** — without rope, usually no higher than the height from which the climber would feel safe jumping off. Landings are protected by **crash pads** and a **spotter**.

Bouldering offers a controlled medium for trying, falling, and trying again. World-class boulderers attempt sequences far harder than the hardest roped routes — without the gear-management overhead (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 284).

## Why boulder

- **Pure movement** — no rope, no gear, no anchor.
- **Trying without consequence** (within reasonable height).
- **Training**: builds the contact strength and footwork that transfer directly to roped climbing.
- **Social** — short attempts, easy to share with a group.

## Equipment

- **Climbing shoes** — typically aggressive, tight, for sensitivity.
- **Chalk bag**.
- **Crash pad** — 4–6 inch foam, often stacked or arranged below a problem. No serious boulderer skips this.
- **Spotter** — a partner whose job is to redirect a falling climber away from rock or onto the pad.

## Highball bouldering

Problems more than 15–20 feet high. The mental commitment approaches free soloing. Highball regulars stack multiple pads, position multiple spotters, or top-rope first before going for it (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 284).

## V system

The bouldering grading scale — **V0–V14+**, established at Hueco Tanks, TX in the early 1990s. V0 ≈ 5.10a; V14 ≈ 5.15a (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 312).

## Bouldering as training

The book's stance: bouldering is the single best training for climbing power. Beginners can master the fundamentals in months; intermediates can become experts in a year with regular bouldering (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 284).

## Injury risk

Despite the low height, more broken bones come from bouldering than from roped climbing — ankles, wrists, even spine if a fall goes wrong (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 273). Pad placement, spotter awareness, and *not* attempting wet / mossy / loose holds are essential safety practices.
