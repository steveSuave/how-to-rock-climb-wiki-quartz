> **Needs verification (2010 source)**: bolt-standards content below reflects practice as of the book's 2010 edition. Hardware norms have shifted since — 1/2″ stainless expansion bolts and glue-ins are more dominant than 3/8″ Rawl, and cold-shut lowering anchors have been widely replaced by rings, mussy hooks, and quicklinks. Re-verify against a current source before relying on specific recommendations here.

## Anatomy

A modern climbing bolt consists of:
- **Bolt body** — 3/8" or 1/2" diameter steel rod with an expansion sleeve.
- **Hanger** — a flat metal plate the carabiner clips to.
- **Nut** that tightens the sleeve against the hole walls.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 146–148)

## Standards

| Diameter | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **1/4"** | Old (1960s–70s). Many on classic American slabs. **Suspect** — never lower or rappel directly off one if avoidable. |
| **3/8"** | Modern minimum. 5-piece Rawl Power-Bolt is the gold standard. |
| **1/2"** | Heavy-duty; some recent sport routes. |

**Glue-in bolts** (a smooth-shank bolt set with epoxy) are increasingly common in soft rock (sandstone, limestone) where expansion bolts would crack the rock.

## Hangers

| Hanger | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **SMC (stainless)** | Many failed. *Don't trust* old SMC hangers. |
| **Modern stainless steel** | Bomber. Most current sport routes. |
| **Plated steel** | Common, but rusts in coastal / wet environments. |
| **Leeper** | Recalled 1980s — *never trust*. |
| **Camp USA, Petzl, Fixe** | Current quality producers. |

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 148–151)

## Anti-patterns

### American Triangle
A sling tied into a triangle through both bolt hangers — load multiplies sideways on the hangers. **Bad rigging**; always avoid. Use a single sling with both ends clipped, or a [[rigging-systems|cordelette]] / quad (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 151, 255).

### Cold shut hangers
A relatively soft metal ring that can be closed with a hammer blow. **Notoriously unreliable** at high loads — commonly found atop short sport climbs as a lowering anchor. Lower through *welded* cold shuts only; replace if open (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 152).

### Single-bolt anchor
Never lower or rappel from a single bolt. *Two bolts, opposed locking biners or a [[rigging-systems|quad]]*, always.

## Inspecting a bolt

- **Visual** — hanger flush against the rock? Bolt body straight? No rust streaks?
- **Tug** — wiggle the hanger. A loose hanger means a loose bolt.
- **Tap** — a piton hammer on the hanger should ring, not thud.

If in doubt, back up the bolt with a nut. (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 148)

## Bolt placement (briefly)

The book does *not* teach bolting — that's a specialist activity. Modern bolts require a hammer drill, the right bit, and patience. Bolting style varies by area and is governed by local ethics: some areas allow ground-up bolting on lead, others restrict bolting to rappel placement on existing routes only.

If you find yourself bolting, read the **American Safe Climbing Association** materials and consult local route developers (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 305).
