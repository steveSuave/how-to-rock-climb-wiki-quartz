## The Golden Rule

> An anchor system is not good enough unless it can withstand the greatest force that can possibly be put to it, known as a [[factor-2-fall|factor 2]] fall.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 173)

## Functions

A belay anchor must:
1. Hold the belayer during a fall on the next pitch.
2. Hold a [[factor-2-fall|factor 2]] if the leader falls before placing the first piece.
3. Hold a follower who pitches off a traverse.
4. Provide a convenient power point for tying in the second.

## Construction

A typical anchor consists of:
- **3–4 primary placements** ([[passive-protection|nuts]], [[camming-devices|cams]], [[bolts]], [[natural-anchors|natural]] features).
- **A rigging system** ([[cordelette]], sliding X, equalette, quad) linking them to a single power point.
- **Tie-in to the power point** via figure-eight-on-a-bight or clove hitch.

See [[rigging-systems]].

## The step-by-step process

From the book (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 191):

1. On popular routes, the belay stance is usually well-established. Belay there.
2. Narrow the site down to the most secure, ergonomic position.
3. Locate suitable cracks for the anchor.
4. Set the most bombproof, primary, big nut or cam — preferably multidirectional — and tie off before yelling "Off belay!"
5. Determine the [[direction-of-pull]] for the climber following the pitch and the leader casting off on the next lead.
6. Simply and efficiently shore up with secondary anchors.
7. Set secondaries in close, not cramped, proximity.
8. If the rock is less than perfect, spread anchors out across several features.
9. Connect with modern rigging to safeguard against all possible directions of pull.
10. Tie into the most bombproof anchor with a clove hitch (aids adjustability).
11. When bringing up a second, situate body in line between anchors and direction of pull (**ABC**).
12. **KISS** — Keep It Simple, Stupid.

## SRENE

The anchor should be:
- **S**olid
- **R**edundant
- **E**qualized
- **N**o **E**xtension

See [[srene]] for details. The criteria can never be perfectly met; the value is as an evaluation strategy (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 186).

## The Jesus nut

The first piece *above* the belay anchor — the [[jesus-nut]] — is the most critical placement on the entire pitch. If it holds, the belay anchor never sees factor 2 forces (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 175–176).

## Spreading the load

A fall generates forces; spread them across multiple sound placements (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 193). The primary function is to *hold no matter what*. Equalization is secondary to redundancy.

## Upward oppositionals

Required when (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 211):
- Belayer is significantly lighter than the leader.
- Belaying below an overhang with the Jesus nut directly above or behind.
- Rock is steep enough that a leader fall can lift the belayer > 18".

Place a [[passive-protection|nut]] or [[camming-devices|cam]] set for upward pull below the main downward-pull placements.

## Tying into the anchor

Standard: **figure-eight-on-a-bight** clipped into the power point with a locking biner or two opposed regular biners (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 192). Back up the tie-in by clove-hitching to a separate piece of the anchor.

For multipitch with one climber leading every pitch, the cordelette / equalette is preferred. When the leads swing, tying in with the climbing rope directly to each anchor piece (clove hitches) is faster.
