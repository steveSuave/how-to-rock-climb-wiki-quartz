## Grips

### Open grip
Fingers laid across an edge or in a pocket, second joint extended, hand flat against the wall. The default grip on jugs and rounded holds. Easiest on tendons (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 28–29).

### Crimp
First knuckle hyperextended, second knuckle flexed, fingertips on a small edge. Thumb often locks over the index finger ("full crimp"). The strongest grip on tiny holds and the most likely to injure A2/A4 pulley tendons. Half-crimp (thumb off) is safer (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 30).

### Pocket
Two-finger (bi-doigt) or one-finger (mono-doigt) holes in the rock. Pocket-pulling is exhausting and common on limestone/dolomite/tuff (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 30–31).

### Pinch
Thumb opposed to fingers on a protruding feature. Strength comes from the thumb (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 32).

### Vertical grip
Hand vertical on a hold whose useful edge points sideways, fingers wrapped around. Often used on chickenheads, knobs (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 32–33).

### Ring grip
Fingers nestled close, thumb wrapped over the index finger.

### Sidepull
A hold useful only when pulled sideways. Body must counter-pull from the opposite direction with the feet (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 35–37).

### Undercling
Hold gripped palm-up; body must be pressed up against it to weight it. Often used as a balancing tactic while a free hand reaches above.

### Gaston
A sidepull where the hand is in a thumb-*down* orientation, like trying to pry open elevator doors. Awkward on the shoulder.

### Mantling
A press-down move: hands flat on a ledge, the climber palms upward into a stand. Used to surmount mantleshelves (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 33–34).

## Posture rules

- Hang from a *straight* arm whenever possible. A bent arm fatigues many times faster (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 50).
- Twist the body to put the load through bone, not muscle.
- Move from one stance to the next; don't grovel between holds.
