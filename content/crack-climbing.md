## The crack-climbing mindset

A face climber sees holds. A crack climber sees a *fracture* and asks: *what part of my body cams in this width?* Then he places that body part and weights it. This is counterintuitive at first — there is nothing to grab, only something to wedge (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 49).

## Footwork in cracks

Just as critical as the jam. Boot-jam variants (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 53–55, 60–61):
- **Toe-cam** — boot inserted thumb-down, then twisted thumb-up.
- **Heel-toe** — heel and toe wedged perpendicular to the crack walls.
- **Foot stack (T-bar)** — both feet stacked for off-widths.

Stiff shoes for thin cracks; soft shoes for face moves between cracks.

## Direction of travel

When the crack is parallel-sided, place jams *above your eye line* so your body weight loads them downward. Step the feet up after, leapfrogging.

## Liebacking as alternative

If a crack is a corner with a positive lip, [[lieback]]ing — pulling sideways while pushing the feet against the opposite wall — can be faster than jamming, but more strenuous and harder to protect.

## Protection

Cracks are where most [[passive-protection]] and [[camming-devices]] sit. The placement is usually obvious from a glance, but the trick is to protect *before* the crux jam, not while pumping out on it.

## Skin

Crack climbing destroys skin. Tape the backs of the hands and the bases of the fingers (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 84–85).
