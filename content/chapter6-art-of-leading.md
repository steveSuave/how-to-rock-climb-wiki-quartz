## The first lead

Gym leads with bolted lines are the safest first lead. The transition to an outdoor sport climb (5.9 or easy 5.10 with hangers in place) is incremental; the next step is an easy trad lead at 5.3–5.4 with a [[toprope]] backup if available (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 215–216).

## Guidebooks and the rack

A **topo** is the minimal blueprint of a route — features, fixed anchors, bolt count, difficulty, [[yosemite-decimal-system]] grade. A **rack** is the gear the climber carries; an 80%-of-the-time catchall rack is a full set of wires, cams to 2–3", five or six shoulder runners, ten quickdraws, and a nut tool (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 217–218).

Rack on harness gear loops or a shoulder sling, sorted by size, gates facing the body. Standardize the method (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 220).

## Voice signals

Standard climbing signals from the book (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 221):

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ON BELAY? | Climber asks if belay is ready |
| BELAY ON | Belayer confirms |
| CLIMBING / CLIMB | Going / acknowledged |
| SLACK | Give rope |
| UP ROPE | Take in rope |
| TENSION / TAKE | Hold me tight |
| LOWER | Lower me |
| WATCH ME | Pay close attention |
| FALLING! | Statement of fact |
| BELAY OFF | Climber unties / anchored |
| OFF BELAY! | Belayer acknowledges |
| ROCK! | Falling rock — take cover |
| ROPE! | Rope coming down |

## Placing protection and direction of pull

The first piece off the deck must be solid and ideally multidirectional, so the [[direction-of-pull]] doesn't yank it free as the leader moves up. Long runners extend gear placed off the plumb line so the rope tracks straight up — minimizing rope drag and reducing sideways force on the placements (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 222–224).

Carry a quickdraw on every nut; clip the rope through the runner with the gate facing away from the direction of travel.

## Falling

A leader fall on bomber pro is not inherently dangerous. Stay relaxed, face the rock, legs bent and apart to absorb impact like shock absorbers (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 228). After a 20+ foot fall, lower down and wait ten minutes for the rope to recover stretch before re-leading. See [[falling]].

## Setting the next belay

Two options after leading a pitch (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 233–234):
- **Redirected belay** — rope runs from belayer's harness device, up through a biner on the anchor, then down to the climber. Doubles the load through the "pulley effect" but is comfortable.
- **Direct belay** — belay device clipped directly to the anchor (Reverso in autolock mode, ATC Guide). Anchor takes the bulk of the fall. Preferred by guides; standard in Europe.

## Changing leads

The follower clips into the anchor with two biners (always more than one) before the leader takes them off belay. The leader unties from the anchor; the second begins leading from the same end of the rope (or the team swaps ends if they prefer).

## Double-rope technique

Two thinner ropes (9 mm) clipped alternately reduce drag on wandering routes, halve the fall distance during clipping, and provide a built-in rappel line. Standard in continental Europe and Britain; never caught on in the US (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 236–238).

## Retreat and self-rescue

The most common retreat is lowering off — never lower off a single nut. Bomber backup is mandatory because the rest of the gear is being removed beneath you. Prusik/Bachmann knots enable rope ascent if a leader gets stuck mid-pitch (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 239–240).

## The mental game

Calm yourself, take a reality test, visualize the sequence, and program your subconscious before launching off the stance. Climbing "unconsciously" — a trance-like, alert state — is the goal (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 241).

## Style

Free climbing's spirit: ascend a route without weighting the gear. Definitions (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 243):
- **On-sight flash** — top, no falls, no prior beta.
- **Flash** — top, no falls, with beta.
- **Redpoint** — clean lead after rehearsal.
- **Pinkpoint** — clean lead with gear pre-placed.
- **Hangdog** — hanging on gear between attempts (rehearsal).
