## Finger jams

### Tip / fingertip lock
Thumb-down: rotate the hand so the thumb points down, slide fingers into the crack to the first knuckle, twist back (thumb-up). The twist cams the fingers (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 50).

### Pinky lock
Pinky-first into the crack, then the rest of the fingers stacked behind it. Used in very thin cracks.

### Finger jam (full)
Fingers in to the second knuckle, thumb tucked into the palm or used as a stack. Thumb-down jams are usually more secure for downward force; thumb-up for upward force.

## Off-finger jams (thumb stacks)

Cracks wider than fingers but narrower than a hand. The jam sequences (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 55–57):
- **Thumb stack** — thumb in first, then fingers stacked over the thumbnail, all pulled down.
- **Butterfly** — both hands thumb-to-thumb in opposition, fingers fanning outward.

These hurt. Practice off-fingers progressively.

## Hand jams

The most secure jam there is. Slide the hand into the crack, palm pressed against one wall, thumb tucked across the palm to "cup" the hand into a wedge ("**tripod**"). For wider cracks, "cammed" hand — thumb behind the fingers, fist-shape (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 58–60).

### Cupped vs. cammed
- **Cupped hand** — for slightly-wider-than-hand cracks. Thumb across palm; the hand bridges the gap.
- **Cammed hand** — for hands-plus cracks. Thumb stacked under the index; closing the hand makes the jam tighter.

## Fist jam

Hand closed, knuckles on one wall, heel of hand on the other. Insert with the thumb tucked inside or outside (depending on orientation). Less secure than a hand jam — easier to slip out, harder to twist — but the right tool when the crack is fist-wide (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 62, 64).

## Footwork

| Crack width | Foot technique |
| --- | --- |
| Thin / fingers | Toe-cam: insert thumb-down, twist thumb-up |
| Hand | Standard boot-jam: rotate the foot in, weight the heel |
| Fist | Full foot-jam: more of the boot inside the crack |

## Direction

Place jams **above your head** so your weight loads them downward (which is the direction they were designed to hold). Stepping the feet up *after* the hand jam is set is the basic sequence (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 49–50).
