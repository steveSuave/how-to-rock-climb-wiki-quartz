## Static vs. automatic equalization

Two classes (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 194):

- **Static (pre-equalized)** — pieces tied off with no slack or adjustability. Distributes load in *one anticipated direction* only. Example: cordelette.
- **Automatic (self-equalizing / dynamic)** — sling configuration shifts to redistribute load as the direction of pull changes. Example: sliding X.

Composite anchors combine both. Limiter knots on a sliding X create a hybrid.

## Cordelette

The mainstream rigging tool. An 18-foot loop of 7 mm nylon (double fisherman's) or 5.5 mm high-tensile cord (triple fisherman's) (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 195).

**Rigging:**
1. Clip the cordelette through each of the 2–4 primary placements.
2. Pull the loops down between each pair of pieces.
3. Pull all loops tight in the anticipated direction of pull.
4. Tie an overhand or figure-eight on a bight to form the power point.
5. Clip locking biners through the power point loop.

**Strengths:**
- Quick to rig once practiced.
- Works well with 2 equal-length arms (sport top-anchor on 2 bolts).
- One cordelette covers most situations.

**Weaknesses:**
- Static equalization — works only for the *one anticipated* direction.
- Equalization is imperfect when arms are unequal length; the shortest arm takes most of the load (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 198–199).
- Stretchy nylon helps; high-tensile cord doesn't.

## Sliding X

A sling twisted into an X between two pieces. Both biners clip through the X, so the sling self-adjusts to any pull direction (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 200).

**Rigging:**
1. Clip a sling through both pieces.
2. *Twist one strand* between the pieces (this is the critical step — a missed twist means total failure if one piece blows).
3. Clip a biner through both strands of the X.
4. Done.

**Strengths:**
- Automatic equalization.
- Fast: ~30 seconds to rig.

**Weaknesses:**
- Extension if one piece fails (the sling slides).
- Limit knots reduce extension but also reduce equalization.
- Best between two pieces; bad for 3+.

## Limiter knots

Overhand knots tied in the sling on each side of the X, just above the biner. Limits how far the sling can slide if a piece fails (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 200). Standard practice on a sliding X.

## Equalette

A doubled cord with two limiter knots ~10 inches apart in the middle. The middle becomes the power point; two locking biners clip the two strands between the knots (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 203–204).

**Rigging:**
1. Use 20 ft of 7 mm nylon cord, tied in a loop.
2. Form a U shape.
3. Tie limiter knots ~10 inches apart in the middle of the U.
4. Connect the two arms to placements via clove hitches, overhand knots, or loops.
5. Power point: two lockers in the two strands between the knots.

**Strengths:**
- Better equalization than a cordelette when arms are unequal.
- Handles 3–4 placements.
- Limited extension by the limiter knots.
- Power point swings to accommodate side load.

**Weaknesses:**
- Takes practice to rig fluidly.
- Bulkier than a cordelette to break down.

## Quad

A doubled equalette — four strands of cord between two limiter knots. The favored rigging for two side-by-side bolts (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 206).

**Rigging:**
1. Use 20 ft of 5.5 mm high-tensile cord, tied in a loop.
2. Double the loop (now four strands).
3. Form into a U.
4. Tie limiter knots ~10 inches apart, with four strands between.
5. Clip the doubled ends to the two bolts.
6. Power point: two lockers in 2–3 of the four strands between the knots.

**Strengths:**
- Near-perfect equalization for two equal placements.
- Almost instantaneous to clip.
- Pre-rigged on a sling, becomes a ready-made sport anchor.

**Weaknesses:**
- Specialized for two pieces.
- Static cord — relies on the dual biners and sliding strands for any adjustment.

## Combinations

Common composites (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 207–208):
- **Sliding X + cordelette** — two pieces on a sliding X, then a cordelette to a third piece. Sliding X equalizes the pair; cordelette adds the third to the power point.
- **Equalette + sliding X** — extra pieces connected via sliding X to an arm of the equalette.

## Which to use when

| Situation | Best rigging |
| --- | --- |
| Two equal-length bolts on a sport climb | Quad |
| Two bolts at uneven distance | Equalette |
| Two cracks side by side with a few pieces | Cordelette |
| Direction of pull uncertain | Equalette or sliding X with limiter knots |
| Quick trad belay with the climbing rope | Direct clove hitches to each piece — no separate rigging |
