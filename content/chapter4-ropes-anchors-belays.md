## The safety chain

Every component of the safety chain — rope, harness, knot, biner, pro, anchor, belayer — must hold (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 88). A failure anywhere ends the chain. The book methodically introduces each link.

## Ropes

Modern climbing ropes are kernmantle nylon, UIAA-tested for dynamic stretch and a maximum impact force of 12 kN (~2,640 lbf) on an 80 kg dummy. Lengths: 50, 60, 70 m (200- and 230-foot lines now common). Diameters from 9.2 mm sport doubles to 11 mm workhorses (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 89–94). See [[rope]].

## Webbing and cordage

Tubular nylon webbing (1 inch) and high-tensile cords (Spectra / Dyneema / Dynex) substitute for rope at anchors. Spectra is strong but slippery — knots can slip — and intolerant of heat (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 94–97). See [[webbing-and-cordage]].

## Knots

The essential knots:
- **Figure-eight follow-through** — standard tie-in to harness.
- **Water knot (ring bend)** — joining ends of webbing.
- **Double fisherman's (grapevine)** — joining ropes or cord; the standard tie for [[cordelette]] loops.
- **Figure-eight on a bight** — clip-in loop at anchor.
- **Clove hitch** — adjustable tie-in to a power point.
- **Bowline-on-a-coil**, **overhand-on-a-bight**, **Munter hitch** — situational.
See [[knots]].

## Harness, biners, hardware

Modern [[harness-and-tie-in]] is a sewn sit harness with belay loop. [[carabiners]] come in oval / D / asym D / pear shapes, with wire, bent, or screw-locking gates. Two opposed regular biners substitute for one locker in a pinch.

## Anchors and protection

The book walks through:
- **[[natural-anchors]]**: trees (girth-hitched), blocks, horns, flakes, chockstones, threaded tunnels.
- **[[passive-protection]]**: tapers (straight, curved, offset), micro-nuts, Tricams, hexes, Big Bros.
- **[[camming-devices]]**: SLCDs — Camalots, Friends, Power Cams — rigid vs. flexible stems, dangerous placements (too tight, offset, tipped out).
- **[[pitons]]**: knifeblade, Lost Arrow, angle, RURP — rarely placed today, never remove fixed.
- **[[bolts]]**: 3/8" 5-piece Rawl/Power-Bolt standard; old 1/4" suspect; SMC vs. stainless hangers; American Triangle bad.

Tom Cecil's **SOS** acronym (Solid, Orientation, Surface area) evaluates any placement (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 140).

## The belay

A [[belay-device]] (ATC, Reverso, Grigri) clipped to the harness belay loop with a locking biner provides the friction to catch a fall. The **guide hand** feeds rope; the **brake hand** never leaves the rope. The three-part take-in sequence — pull, pinch, slide — becomes muscle memory after a few hundred reps in the gym (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 160, 167).

Remember **ABC**: Anchor-Belayer-Climber. The belayer must station in a straight line between the direction of pull and the anchor (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 161).
