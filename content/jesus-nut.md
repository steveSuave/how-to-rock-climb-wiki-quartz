## Origin

A Vietnam-era helicopter mechanics' term. The Bell UH-1 "Huey" had a single stainless steel nut that held the main rotor mast to the rotor blades.

> "If it fails, the next person you see will be Jesus."

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 176)

In climbing, it's the first piece off the [[belay-anchor]].

## Why it matters

Every fall the leader takes is held *first* by the topmost piece of pro — the one closest to the leader. As the leader climbs and places more pro, the topmost piece changes. **But the Jesus nut is the topmost piece during the most vulnerable period of the lead**: from the moment the leader leaves the belay until she places the second piece.

If the leader falls before the Jesus nut is placed, the [[belay-anchor]] takes a [[factor-2-fall|factor 2 fall]] — the worst-case load. If the Jesus nut is placed and *blows* during the next fall, the belayer is still tied to the anchor, and a factor 2 fall can again hit the anchor (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 175–177).

## Standards for the Jesus nut

The Jesus nut must be:
1. **Bombproof** — apply the [[srene|SRENE]] criteria as you would for a belay anchor.
2. **Multidirectional** — must hold up *and* down, sideways pulls, swing.
3. **Placed early** — as soon as feasible after leaving the belay. Don't run out 20 feet to "find a better placement."

If a single piece isn't bombproof, *double up*. Two pieces equalized with a sling forms a redundant Jesus nut (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 177).

## "Carpet bombing" strategy

Tom Cecil's name for the practice of placing many pieces early on a lead — overprotection, but worth the carry weight. Beginners should default to this; experts have the judgment to skimp (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 209).

## What if there's no Jesus nut?

When no good placement exists immediately above the belay (off a hanging belay on a soaring corner, off bolted face climbs from the 1970s with the first bolt 15 feet up), the leader is effectively *free soloing* the lower section. The book's term: "all in" (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 177).

Mitigations:
- Spot the leader with the belayer's body braced.
- Anchor the belayer for *upward* pull as well as downward.
- Communicate before the leader leaves the belay.
- Climb the lower section with extreme care.
