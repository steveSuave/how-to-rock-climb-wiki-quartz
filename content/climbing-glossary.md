## A

- **abseiling** — Descending by sliding down a rope. European synonym for [[rappel]].
- **adze** — The flat end of an ice ax head.
- **aid** — Getting up a climb using gear instead of just hands, feet, and body English.
- **aid climbing** — Using equipment for direct assistance, allowing passage over rock otherwise impossible with hands and feet only.
- **aid route** — A route ascendable only via aid.
- **alcove** — A belay ledge surrounded by vertical rock on all sides.
- **Alien** — Brand of SLCD ([[camming-devices]]).
- **anchor** — A means of securing climbers to the cliff; the point where the rope is fixed. See [[belay-anchor]].
- **anchor matrix** — The placement of anchors using various rigging systems. See [[rigging-systems]].
- **arête** — A narrow ridge or outside edge of rock.
- **arm bar** — A wide-crack technique; see [[off-width-techniques]].
- **arm lock** — See arm bar.
- **ATC** — Air Traffic Controller; a [[belay-device]] by Black Diamond.

## B

- **backstep** — Climbing move; outside edge of the foot behind, usually on a vertical hold. See [[footwork]].
- **bail** — To give up on a climb due to weather, conditions, or judgment.
- **bashie** — A piece of malleable metal hammered into a seam; aid only.
- **belay** — Procedure of securing a climber with a rope. See [[belay]].
- **belayer** — The person at the belay station securing the climber.
- **beta** — Prior information about a climb — sequence, rests, gear, clips.
- **beta flash** — Leading a climb without falling or dogging, but with prior knowledge.
- **bi-doigt** — A two-finger handhold ([[handholds|pocket]]).
- **Big Bro** — Specialized [[passive-protection|protection]] for wide cracks.
- **Big Dude** — Brand of SLCD ([[camming-devices]]).
- **big wall** — A long climb traditionally done over multiple days.
- **bight** — A loop, as in a bight of rope.
- **biner** — See [[carabiners]].
- **birdbeak** — A tiny hooked [[pitons|piton]] by A5.
- **bivi / bivouac** — An uncomfortable sleeping place in the middle of a route.
- **blast** — To begin a big wall after the line fixing is done.
- **bolt** — An artificial anchor placed in a drilled hole. See [[bolts]].
- **bomber / bombproof** — Absolutely fail-safe (anchor, hold).
- **bong** — Extinct extra-wide [[pitons|piton]]; today large chocks are used.
- **bouldering** — Climbing short, hard problems without a rope. See [[bouldering]].
- **bounce** — To crater from extreme height; usually lethal.
- **brain bucket** — A helmet.
- **bridge** — See stem.
- **bucket** — A handhold large enough to fully latch.
- **bump** — Using an intermediate hold briefly before reaching higher.
- **buttress** — An outside edge of rock, much broader than an arête.

## C

- **cam** — To lodge in a crack by counterpressure (verb); to use a [[camming-devices|SLCD]] (noun).
- **Camalot** — Brand of SLCD by Black Diamond ([[camming-devices]]).
- **campus** — To climb overhanging rock with arms only; a [[training|training]] method.
- **carabiner** — Aluminum alloy snap-link. See [[carabiners]].
- **ceiling** — A horizontal section of rock above your head; a roof.
- **chalk** — Magnesium carbonate used to absorb hand sweat. See [[equipment]].
- **chickenhead** — A bulbous knob of rock.
- **chimney** — A crack wide enough to fit the body. See [[chimney-and-stem]].
- **chock** — See nut ([[passive-protection]]).
- **chockstone** — A rock lodged in a crack; a [[natural-anchors|natural anchor]].
- **choss / chossy** — Rotten, fracturing rock.
- **Class 1–6** — Mountain travel classification. See [[yosemite-decimal-system]].
- **clean** — Routes free of vegetation or loose rock; act of removing chocks placed by a leader.
- **cliff** — A high, steep, or overhanging face.
- **cling grip** — Handhold grasped with the fingers.
- **cold shut** — A relatively soft metal ring closed with a hammer blow; suspect anchor.
- **cordelette** — Standard tackle for equalizing 2+ anchors. See [[cordelette]].
- **crack** — A type of irregularity on the stone.
- **crag** — A steep, rugged mass of rock projecting upward or outward.
- **crimp** — Upper-body grip with the first knuckle extended. See [[handholds]].
- **crimper** — A small but positive sharp edge.
- **crux** — The most difficult move or sequence on a climb.

## D

- **dihedral** — An inside corner of climbing surface, like the open angle of a book.
- **downclimb / downclimbing** — A rope-less descent. See [[chapter7-getting-down]].
- **drag** — Resistance of rope running through carabiners.
- **dynamic** — A lunge or explosive leap for a hold.
- **dynamo / dyno / dyne** — See dynamic.

## E

- **edge** — A small hold; the act of standing on one.
- **edging** — Climbing move; placing the very edge of the shoe on a hold. See [[footwork]].
- **exposure** — A relative situation where a climb has noticeable sheerness.

## F

- **flag** — Using a limb as a counterbalance.
- **flake** — Type of irregularity on stone.
- **flash** — Free climbing a route bottom-to-top on the first try, with beta.
- **footwork** — The art and method of standing on holds. See [[footwork]].
- **free / free climb / free ascent** — Upward progress gained by a climber's own efforts; ropes only safeguard, not aid. See [[lead-climbing]].
- **free solo** — Free climbing alone from bottom to top on the first try. See [[free-soloing]].
- **Friends** — Original SLCDs by Wild Country ([[camming-devices]]).
- **frog step** — Climbing move with both feet up while torso stays low ("bullfrog"). See [[footwork]].

## G

- **gaston** — A handhold above and to the side, hand in thumbs-down position. See [[handholds]].
- **girth hitch** — Method of looping a sling around a tree or horn.
- **gobies** — Hand abrasions.
- **grade** — A rating of overall seriousness, denoted by Roman numerals I–VI. See [[yosemite-decimal-system]].
- **Grigri** — Auto-locking [[belay-device]] by Petzl.
- **gripped** — Extremely scared.
- **greasy** — A slick surface.

## H

- **hangdog / hangdogging** — Climbing a route bolt-to-bolt with rope assistance.
- **headwall** — A steeper section of cliff toward the top.
- **heel hooking** — Using the foot as a hand with the heel hooked over a hold. See [[footwork]].
- **hex / hexentric** — Six-sided [[passive-protection|nut]].
- **highball** — Bouldering high off the ground.
- **horn** — A flake-like projection of rock; [[natural-anchors|natural anchor]].

## J

- **jam** — Wedging body parts into a crack. See [[jamming]].
- **jug** — A handhold like a jug handle.

## K

- **kneedrop / drop knee** — Climbing move with the knee dropped low. See [[footwork]].
- **krab** — See carabiner ([[carabiners]]).

## L

- **latch** — To successfully grip a hold.
- **layback / liebacking** — Climbing maneuver pulling sideways with hands while pushing with feet. See [[lieback]].
- **lead** — To climb first, belayed from below, placing protection. See [[lead-climbing]].
- **line** — The path of the route.
- **lock-off** — A single handhold strong enough to hold while the other hand moves.
- **lunge** — An out-of-control [[dynamic-climbing|dynamic]] move.

## M

- **manky** — Of poor quality (jam, placement).
- **mantle / mantling** — Climbing maneuver of pressing up on a ledge. See [[handholds]].
- **micro-taper** — Small [[passive-protection|taper]] for aid climbing.
- **mo** — See dynamic.
- **mono-doigt** — A one-finger pocket. See [[handholds]].
- **mountaineering** — Reaching mountaintops using a combination of skills.
- **move** — Movement; one of a series in a climb.
- **Munter hitch** — [[belay-device|Belay method]] requiring no special device.

## N

- **nut** — Wedge or mechanical device for [[passive-protection|protection]].

## O

- **off-width** — Cracks too wide for fingers, hands, or fist but too narrow for body. See [[off-width-techniques]].
- **on-sight** — Climbing a route without prior knowledge.
- **on-sight flash** — See on-sight.
- **open grip** — Hand grip with fingers extended. See [[handholds]].
- **opposition** — Holds held in place by simultaneous opposing forces.

## P

- **passive nut** — Nonmechanical chock. See [[passive-protection]].
- **pegs** — See [[pitons]].
- **pinch grip** — Hand grip where thumb opposes fingers. See [[handholds]].
- **pinkpoint** — Clean lead with quickdraws pre-placed. See [[sport-climbing]].
- **pitch** — The section of rope between belays.
- **piton** — A metal spike hammered into the rock. See [[pitons]].
- **placement** — The position of a nut or anchor.
- **pocket** — A hole in the surface used as a hold. See [[handholds]].
- **pro / protection** — Anchors safeguarding the leader. See [[passive-protection]], [[camming-devices]], [[bolts]].
- **Prusik** — Knot and method of mechanically ascending a rope. See [[knots]].
- **pulling plastic** — Indoor wall climbing.
- **pumpy** — Indicating continuous, forearm-pumping climbing.

## Q

- **Quad Cam** — Brand of SLCD ([[camming-devices]]).
- **quickdraw** — Short sling with biners at each end for [[lead-climbing|lead]] protection.

## R

- **rack** — The climbing gear carried on a climb.
- **rappel** — To descend a rope using a friction device. See [[rappel]].
- **redpoint** — A clean lead with no falls or rests. See [[sport-climbing]].
- **rib** — A narrow buttress, less sharp than an arête.
- **ring grip** — A handhold with fingers nestled and thumb over the index.
- **Rocks** — Brand of [[passive-protection|passive crack protection]].
- **roof** — A horizontal section of rock above one's head; ceiling.
- **RPs** — Type of micro-[[passive-protection|taper]].
- **R-rated climb** — Serious-injury potential; sparse protection.
- **runner** — See sling.
- **runout** — A long stretch of climbing without protection.

## S

- **sandbagging** — Underestimating a route's actual difficulty.
- **second** — The second climber on a rope team; usually the leader's belayer.
- **send it** — Encouragement to finish a route without falling.
- **sharp end** — The lead climber's end of the rope.
- **shred** — To do really well.
- **sidepull** — A vertically-oriented handhold pulled to the side. See [[handholds]].
- **signals** — Voice commands between climbers. See [[belay]].
- **slab** — A section of rock with gentle angle, less than vertical.
- **slap** — To touch a hold but fail to latch.
- **sling** — A webbing loop. See [[webbing-and-cordage]].
- **smear / smearing** — Standing on the front of the foot with rubber pressed flat. See [[footwork]].
- **soft ratings** — Ratings easier than actual difficulty.
- **solo climbing** — Climbing without a rope. See [[free-soloing]].
- **Spectra** — High-tensile climbing rope/cord material.
- **sport climbing** — Climbing on bolted routes. See [[sport-climbing]].
- **spotter** — A person who slows a boulderer's fall.
- **spring-loaded camming device (SLCD)** — See [[camming-devices]].
- **stance** — A standing rest spot, often the site of a belay.
- **static step** — Climbing move pressing weight on one leg. See [[footwork]].
- **stem / stemming** — Counterpressing with the feet between widely spaced holds. See [[chimney-and-stem]].
- **stopper / stopper knot** — Brand of [[passive-protection|nut]]; also a knot in the rope end.
- **sustained** — Indicating continuous difficulty on a climb.

## T

- **taper** — A boxy-shaped [[passive-protection|chock]].
- **TCU** — See three-cam unit.
- **thin** — Featureless climb or hold.
- **three-cam unit (TCU)** — Three-lobed [[camming-devices|SLCD]] for thin cracks.
- **thrutch** — To strain excessively to move up.
- **toeing-in** — Pointing the toe onto a hold; useful in pockets.
- **topo** — A topographic line drawing of a climb. See [[chapter6-art-of-leading]].
- **toprope / toproping** — Belay from an anchor above. See [[toprope]].
- **trad / traditional rock climbing** — Roped climbing with placed [[passive-protection|protection]].
- **traverse** — To move sideways without altitude gain.
- **Tricam** — [[passive-protection|Brand of anchor]] that creates a tripod inside a crack.
- **tweak** — To injure.

## U

- **undercling** — Grabbing a hold with the palm up. See [[handholds]].

## V

- **V system** — Bouldering grade system V0–V14+. See [[yosemite-decimal-system]].

## W

- **wall** — A long climb; see [[bouldering|big wall]].
- **water knot** — Used to tie a [[webbing-and-cordage|webbing loop]]. See [[knots]].
- **wired** — Known well, as in "wired route."
- **work / worked / working** — Practicing the moves of a route via toprope or hangdogging.

## X

- **X-rated climb** — Climb with groundfall and death potential.

## Y

- **YDS** — See [[yosemite-decimal-system]].
