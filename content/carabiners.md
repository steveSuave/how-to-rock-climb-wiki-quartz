## Shapes

| Shape | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Oval** | Symmetric. Inefficient (load distributes evenly across spine and gate side). Cheap; used in carabiner-brake rappel. |
| **D** | Asymmetric — load biased to the spine. Stronger for same weight. |
| **Asymmetric D** | Most popular all-purpose biner today. |
| **Pear (HMS)** | Large, wide-mouthed, locking. For [[belay-device|belay devices]] and [[knots|Munter hitches]]. |

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 105)

## Gates

| Gate | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Straight gate** | Standard for racking and quickdraw bolt-end. |
| **Bent gate** | Curved gate that "scoops" the rope in for clipping. **Only** on the rope-end of a quickdraw — never on a bolt, fixed pin, or wired nut (gate can pop open). |
| **Wire gate** | Lighter, less likely to "gate flutter" under impact. Standard on modern quickdraws. |
| **Locking (screw or auto-lock)** | A sleeve over the gate prevents accidental opening. Required for belay device, rappel device, and any anchor power point. |

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 106, 225)

## Strength

UIAA-rated carabiners are stamped with:
- **Major axis (closed gate)** — typical 22–24 kN.
- **Minor axis (cross-loaded)** — typical 7 kN. Catastrophically weaker.
- **Open gate** — typical 7 kN.

Therefore: keep biners loaded along the **major axis with gate closed**.

## Opposing gates

Two regular carabiners with gates **opposed and reversed** (one gate facing left, the other right) is a redundant substitute for a single locker. It's harder for both gates to be wrenched open at the same time (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 153). Standard practice on the power point of a belay anchor when only one locker is available.

## Quickdraw

A short sewn sling with a regular biner on one end and a bent-gate biner on the rope end. Used to clip the rope to a piece of [[passive-protection]], a [[camming-devices|cam]], or a [[bolts|bolt]]. Standard sport-climbing tool (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 225).

## Maintenance

- Wash with soap and water if salty / sandy. Lubricate the gate hinge.
- Retire if the gate sticks, the spring is weak, or visible cracks appear.
- Retire after any significant drop (microcracks invisible to the eye).

## Clipping rules

1. Clip the rope through the bent-gate biner with the gate facing **away from the direction of travel**.
2. Never let the rope back-clip (running outside-in instead of inside-out) — under tension the rope can unclip itself.
3. Never load over an edge (the biner can lever).
4. Never thread a sling through a wired nut — the wire can saw through (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 224).
