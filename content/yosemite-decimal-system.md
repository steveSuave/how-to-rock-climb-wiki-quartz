## Class (terrain type)

| Class | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Trail hiking |
| 2 | Off-trail / talus; hands occasional |
| 3 | Scrambling; hands needed, careful feet |
| 4 | Steep, exposed scrambling; a rope may be used |
| 5 | Technical free climbing — ropes, protection, hardware. Subdivided by decimal |
| 6 | Aid climbing — gear used for upward progress, denoted A0–A5 (e.g., 5.9/A3) |

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 307)

## The 5.x decimal

Class 5 routes range from **5.0** (easiest) to **5.15** (today's hardest). The decimal jumps were once supposed to be linear, but the scale was open-ended in the 1950s when "5.10" was the supposed limit. As climbers exceeded that limit, 5.10–5.15 was subdivided **a/b/c/d** (e.g., 5.11a, 5.11b, 5.11c, 5.11d). When the grade is uncertain, two letters may be combined: 5.10a/b.

Plus/minus (5.9+, 5.10-) is the coarser, older variant.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 312)

## Grade (time/seriousness)

A separate Roman numeral describes the overall commitment:

| Grade | Time |
| --- | --- |
| I | A few hours, Class 4 scrambles, Class 5 single-pitch |
| II | 3–4 hours |
| III | 4–6 hours, strong half day |
| IV | Full day |
| V | 1–2 days, technical difficulty, bivy usually unavoidable |
| VI | 2+ days on the wall |

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 309)

## Safety ratings

Some guidebooks append:
- **R-rated** — protection sparse or runout; falls may result in injury.
- **X-rated** — groundfall or death potential.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 311)

## Sandbagging and soft ratings

- **Sandbagged** — harder than the rating suggests (common at older areas like Yosemite, Joshua Tree).
- **Soft** — easier than rated (common at newer sport areas).

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 311)

## V system (bouldering)

Parallel to YDS but for [[bouldering]] problems: **V0–V14+**, established at Hueco Tanks, TX in the early 1990s. V0 ≈ 5.10a; V14 ≈ 5.15a (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 312).
