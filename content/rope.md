## Construction

Modern climbing ropes are **kernmantle**: a braided protective sheath (*mantle*) wraps a load-bearing nylon core (*kern*). Earlier ropes — hemp, then "Perlon" laid lines — were either weak, stiff, or both (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 90). The kernmantle design has been the standard since the 1960s.

## UIAA specifications

The **Union Internationale des Associations d'Alpinisme** (UIAA) drop-tests ropes with an 80 kg weight on 2.5 m of rope, dropped 5 m (a [[factor-2-fall|factor 2 fall]]). Required performance:

- **Maximum impact force**: < 12 kN (~2,640 lbf).
- **Dynamic stretch under load**: 6–8%.
- **Minimum number of falls held**: ≥ 5 before failure.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 91–92)

A UIAA-approved rope is what every climber should buy.

## Common dimensions

| Length | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 50 m (165 ft) | Old standard; short trad and sport |
| 60 m | Current default; most modern sport routes |
| 70 m | Long sport and modern trad; allows long single-rope rappels |

| Diameter | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 11 mm | Heavy, durable; top-rope mules, big walls |
| 10–10.5 mm | Trad workhorse |
| 9.7–9.9 mm | Modern thin single rope |
| 9.2–9.5 mm | Sport leading on bolts |
| 8–8.5 mm | "Half" or "double" ropes; used in pairs |

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 92–94)

## Fall factor

**Fall factor** = length of fall ÷ length of rope paid out. Range 0–2. A factor 2 fall is the worst case — the rope sees full impact force because there is no rope length to absorb it.

A leader 5 ft above the belay with no pro in falls 10 ft on 5 ft of rope = factor 2.
A leader 50 ft above the belay with pro 45 ft below who falls 10 ft on 50 ft of rope = factor 0.2.

## Care

- Store dry, out of UV.
- Wash with mild soap, air dry.
- Inspect for sheath damage, soft spots in the core.
- After a long fall (20+ ft), lower down and wait ~10 minutes for stretch to recover.
- Retire after ~5 years of regular use, or sooner after a serious fall over a sharp edge.

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 93)

## Static vs. dynamic

A **dynamic** rope stretches to absorb falls — required for any lead climbing.
A **static** rope does not stretch — used for fixed lines, hauling, caving, top-rope rigging when stretch is unwanted.

Never lead on a static rope.
