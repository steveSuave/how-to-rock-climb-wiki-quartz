## Core principles

- **Stand on your feet.** Hands steer, feet propel. The most universal beginner mistake is overgripping with the arms and neglecting precise foot placement (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 17–18).
- **Move from the core.** Power flows from hips and torso. Arms and legs deliver the force the core has set up (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 47).
- **Stay in balance.** Three points of contact, weight stacked over the feet. Twist the hip into the wall (backstep, drop knee) to keep the center of mass close to the rock (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 22–24).

## Footwork

The two basic foot placements are [[footwork|smearing]] (rubber pressed flat to the rock) and [[footwork|edging]] (the edge of the sole on a small hold). Advanced moves: backstepping, drop knee, rest step, spring step, frog step, heel hooking, toeing-in. See [[footwork]] for details.

## Handholds

A vocabulary of grips: open grip, [[handholds|crimp]], pocket (bi-doigt, mono-doigt), pinch, vertical grip, undercling, sidepull, gaston, [[handholds|mantling]]. See [[handholds]].

## Steep face and dynamics

On overhanging rock, [[footwork|backstepping]] and flagging keep the hips close to the wall and free a hand to reach. When holds run too far apart for a static move, climbers turn to [[dynamic-climbing]] — deadpoints, dynos, hucking, powerglides (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 43–47).

## Slab climbing

On low-angle faces (under vertical), friction is everything. Weight goes through smeared feet; the hands balance only. The "tripod" position — feet wide, butt out, arms relaxed — is the basic posture (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 38).
