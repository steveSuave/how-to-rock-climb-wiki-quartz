## Webbing

| Type | Material | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tubular nylon** | Nylon | The traditional sling material. Soft, abrasion-resistant, knots cinch securely. Standard width 1" or 9/16". |
| **Flat (mil-spec) nylon** | Nylon | Older / military style. Cheap. |
| **Spectra** | UHMW polyethylene | 4× the strength-to-weight of nylon. Slippery; knots can slip. Low melting point (~150 °C) — friction burns through it. |
| **Dyneema / Dynex** | UHMW polyethylene | Same as Spectra under different brand names (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 95). |

**Sewn slings** are stronger than hand-tied ones — no knot weakens the loop. Standard sewn-sling lengths: shoulder (60 cm doubled), single (120 cm doubled).

## Knots in webbing

- Tied loops use the **[[knots|water knot]] (ring bend)** — a doubled overhand. The tails must be ≥ 2 inches long; inspect and re-tighten regularly. Tested loops have failed when tails crept through (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 96).
- Tie water knots only in nylon. Never in Spectra — it slips.

## Cordage (accessory cord)

Round cord, sold by diameter:

| Size | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 4 mm | Prusiks, accessory loops |
| 5.5 mm high-tensile | Compact [[cordelette]] for sport top-anchors |
| 6 mm | Tie-offs, light Prusiks |
| 7 mm nylon | Standard [[cordelette]] |
| 8 mm | Heavy-duty rappel Prusik |

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 97–98)

Cord loops are tied with a **double fisherman's** (nylon) or **triple fisherman's** (high-tensile). The triple is needed on Spectra-cored cords because the slick material requires more wraps.

## Standard cordelette

The standard cordelette is an 18-foot loop of 7 mm nylon (double fisherman's) or 5.5 mm high-tensile (triple fisherman's). Used to equalize 2–4 anchor pieces into a single power point (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 195).

See [[rigging-systems]] for how to rig one.

## Why never trust webbing as a daisy chain

A daisy chain — a sewn loop with multiple internal pockets — is *static*. Falling onto a daisy chain shock-loads the body with no give. The Tahquitz Rock double fatality occurred when the belayer was tied to a cordelette with high-tensile cord rather than the dynamic climbing rope (source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, p. 199).

The lesson: **stay connected to the anchor with a length of climbing rope** so the system has dynamic give. Never use a daisy chain for your primary anchor tie-in.
