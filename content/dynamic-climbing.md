## The spectrum

A static move reaches the next hold from a position of balance. A dynamic move uses momentum — the climber leaves the rock briefly, propelled by leg drive, and catches the next hold at the apex.

| Move | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Deadpoint** | Reach to a hold at the instant when upward velocity = 0; gravity briefly cancels. Hand catches just before falling back. |
| **Dyno** | A leaping move; both feet may leave the rock. Aim for a defined target. |
| **Huck** | An all-or-nothing dyno; commit to a far hold or fall. |
| **Powerglide** | Continuous dynamic momentum — climbing fast through a sequence on rhythm rather than balance. |

(source: how-to-rock-climb.pdf, pp. 43–47)

## Mechanics

1. Lower the body — bend the legs deeply.
2. Pull in with the arms while the legs explode upward.
3. Eyes lock on the target hold throughout.
4. At the apex, the upper hand catches with an open grip (locking in mid-motion damages tendons).
5. Recover feet onto the wall.

## When to go dynamic

- Holds beyond static reach.
- Static reach burning too much strength.
- Smooth slick rock where lunging avoids slipping off small footholds.

## Risks

Missing a dyno on lead means a fall the length of the lunge plus rope stretch. On well-protected sport routes, this is unremarkable. On thin trad, it's the difference between sending and a hospital visit.
